Why Nepal is not legalizing crypto?

Author: BachatYatra Team June 2026 5 min read

Introduction

Cryptocurrency has transformed the global financial landscape over the past decade. Countries around the world have adopted different approaches toward digital assets such as Bitcoin, Ethereum, and stablecoins. While some nations have fully embraced cryptocurrencies through regulation, others have imposed strict restrictions or outright bans. Nepal falls into the latter category. Despite increasing global adoption and growing public interest, cryptocurrency remains illegal in Nepal. Many Nepalis continue to ask why the government has not legalized crypto, especially when neighboring and developed countries are gradually building regulatory frameworks instead of banning it altogether. Understanding Nepal's position requires examining its economy, financial system, regulatory challenges, and national priorities.

Nepal's Current Legal Position

As of today, cryptocurrency trading, mining, buying, selling, or facilitating crypto-related transactions is considered illegal in Nepal. The Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB), the country's central bank, has repeatedly issued notices warning citizens against participating in cryptocurrency activities. The government has also taken action against individuals and organizations promoting or operating crypto-related businesses within Nepal. Unlike countries that regulate cryptocurrencies through licensing and taxation, Nepal has chosen prohibition over regulation. This decision is based on several economic and legal concerns rather than opposition to blockchain technology itself.

Protecting Foreign Currency Reserves

One of the biggest reasons Nepal has not legalized cryptocurrency is the protection of its foreign exchange reserves. Nepal is an import-dependent country. It imports petroleum products, electronics, vehicles, medicines, machinery, and many other essential goods. To pay for these imports, Nepal needs foreign currencies such as the US Dollar, Euro, and Indian Rupee. If cryptocurrency were freely available, many people might purchase Bitcoin or other digital assets using foreign currency. This could increase the demand for foreign exchange and reduce Nepal's already limited reserves. Since maintaining sufficient foreign currency is essential for international trade and economic stability, the government views unrestricted crypto trading as a potential financial risk.

Maintaining Monetary Control

Another important concern is the government's ability to control the nation's monetary system. Nepal Rastra Bank manages the country's monetary policy by controlling the supply of money, interest rates, and banking regulations. Cryptocurrencies operate independently of central banks. Bitcoin, for example, is decentralized and cannot be controlled by any government. If a large number of citizens begin using cryptocurrencies instead of the Nepalese Rupee (NPR), the central bank's ability to influence the economy through traditional monetary policies could weaken. Maintaining confidence in the national currency remains a priority for policymakers.

Risk of Money Laundering and Illegal Activities

Cryptocurrencies allow users to transfer funds across borders quickly without relying on traditional banking systems. While this offers legitimate benefits, it also creates opportunities for misuse. Governments worldwide continue to face challenges involving:

  • Money laundering
  • Money laundering
  • Money laundering
  • Money laundering

Although blockchain transactions are publicly recorded, identifying the individuals behind wallet addresses can be difficult without proper regulatory systems. Nepal currently lacks comprehensive cryptocurrency regulations, monitoring infrastructure, and specialized enforcement capabilities necessary to oversee such activities effectively. Rather than attempting to regulate these risks immediately, authorities have chosen to prohibit crypto-related activities.

Investor Protection

Cryptocurrency markets are known for extreme price volatility. Bitcoin has experienced dramatic price swings over the years, sometimes gaining or losing more than 50% of its value within months. Thousands of other cryptocurrencies have risen rapidly before collapsing entirely. Nepal's regulators are concerned that inexperienced investors could lose significant amounts of money by investing in highly speculative digital assets. In recent years, many scams involving fake crypto investment platforms, Ponzi schemes, and fraudulent token launches have affected investors worldwide. By maintaining restrictions, authorities aim to reduce the likelihood of widespread financial losses among the general public.

Conclusion

Nepal's decision not to legalize cryptocurrency is driven primarily by concerns over financial stability, foreign exchange management, investor protection, regulatory readiness, and the prevention of financial crime. While cryptocurrencies offer opportunities for innovation, investment, and faster global transactions, they also introduce significant risks that policymakers believe the country is not yet fully prepared to manage. As technology continues to evolve and international regulatory frameworks become more established, Nepal may eventually transition from prohibition toward carefully regulated adoption. Until then, the government's priority remains safeguarding the nation's financial system and maintaining economic stability. The debate over cryptocurrency in Nepal is far from over. As blockchain technology becomes increasingly integrated into the global economy, the challenge for Nepal will be finding a balance between encouraging innovation and protecting its financial interests. Whether legalization comes in the near future or several years from now, the discussion will continue to shape the country's digital and economic future.
